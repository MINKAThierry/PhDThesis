### Why this study?

Cryptographic evidence (electronic signatures, e-voting proofs, zero-knowledge proofs) is increasingly produced before courts, regulators and auditors. We seek to measure what information these professionals draw from such evidence when they have to reach a decision. The study does not aim to assess your personal competence. It aims to describe trends by professional category.

### Why you?

You currently work as ☐ a judge ☐ a regulator ☐ an auditor ☐ an expert (P2). Your institution has agreed that the invitation be passed on to you. It will not know whether you accepted or declined, except through the contact person in charge of practical organisation.

### What are you asked to do?

For P1, your participation includes the following sessions.

| Session | Content | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Training | Presentation of basic cryptographic notions, without internal technical detail | 2 h |
| Main session | Review of 10 fictitious cases. For each one, you read a context, examine a proof and answer a 30-question questionnaire | 4 h, breaks included |
| Second session | Review of 3 cases again, two weeks later | 1 h |
| Debriefing | Explanation of the study and answers to your questions | 30 min |

For P2, your participation includes ☐ up to four Delphi questionnaires of about ____ min each ☐ one context-comparison session of about ____ h.

All cases are fictitious. Some deliberately contain anomalies. We will not tell you which ones before the debriefing. There is no trick regarding the nature of the study.

### What risks and what benefits?

Risks are minimal. The main session is long and breaks are planned. You will benefit from a 2-hour training and a collective presentation of results. No individual result will be passed on to your institution.

### Compensation

☐ Reimbursement of travel costs on receipt ☐ Flat allowance of ____ FCFA **[to be decided by the team]**. If you withdraw, you keep the share corresponding to the sessions attended.

### Confidentiality and pseudonymisation

- Your name does not appear on any questionnaire. You are given a code (for example `P1-JG-007`).
- The link between your code and your identity is kept in encrypted form by the scientific lead only, separately from the answers.
- The profile questionnaire asks for no name, no date of birth and no institution name. Information is collected in classes (for example "5 to 9 years of seniority").
- Results are published only in aggregated form, by groups of at least ____ persons **[to be decided by the team]**.
- After the anonymisation date, the linking table is destroyed. The anonymised data may then be shared publicly under CC BY 4.0 for research purposes.
- Data are processed in accordance with the Cameroonian framework for the protection of personal data **[reference to be checked by the team]**.

### Voluntary participation and right to withdraw

Your participation is entirely voluntary. You may decline, interrupt a session or withdraw at any time without giving reasons and without any professional consequence. Until ___ / ___ / ______ (anonymisation date, i.e. ____ months after your last session **[to be decided by the team]**), you may request destruction of your data by giving your code to the scientific lead. After that date, your answers can no longer be identified or withdrawn.
